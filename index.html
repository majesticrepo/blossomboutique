Red · Rose

Rose

There is a reason the rose has outlived every fashion. Long before ribbons and boxes, it grew wild along garden walls, folding petal over petal until the centre disappeared into shadow. To hold one is to hold something that has always meant devotion — not the loud kind, but the kind that stays. Ours are cut at first light, when the red is deepest and the scent hasn't yet loosened into the day.

← Back to Orange
Orange · Marigold

Marigold

Marigolds don't ask to be noticed quietly. They open in full, layered rings the colour of late afternoon, and hold that colour through the whole of a bright day. In many homes they're strung across doorways to welcome someone in — a small, cheerful insistence that warmth is always worth making room for.

← Back to Yellow
Yellow · Sunflower

Sunflower

A sunflower spends its whole life turning to face the light, and somehow that simple habit has come to mean hope. Ours grow wide and unashamed, a ring of gold radiating from a heart dark as fertile soil. Carry one into a room and the room remembers what daylight feels like.

← Back to Green
Green · Jade Orchid

Jade Orchid

Tall, pale-green petals, each one cupped like a hand catching rain — the jade orchid carries a quiet, architectural grace that most flowers don't attempt. It grows with no pattern to speak of, only stacked rows of soft colour that arrange themselves like a hushed choir.

← Back to Blue
Blue · Hydrangea

Hydrangea

Hydrangeas bloom in small, tight clusters, each petal packed close like something holding its breath before spring properly arrives. Their blue is the deep, slightly dusty blue of very early morning, and a single stem is enough to make an entire room feel like it's just been rained on, gently.

← Back to Purple
Purple · Lavender

Lavender

Lavender has a way of slowing a room down. Even before you catch the scent, the colour does the work — that soft, spent-violet shade that never quite settles into blue or pink. Traditionally kept among fine linens to travel well, ours are cut long enough to gather into loose, unfussy bunches that look like they've always belonged wherever they're carried.

← Back to Brown
Brown · Chrysanthemum

Chrysanthemum

Chrysanthemums are for the quieter kind of romance — the sort that doesn't need to be the brightest thing in the room. Their petals hold the colour of well-worn leather and late autumn light, warm rather than dark. Rare among our colourways, this one has become something of a favourite for those who prefer their elegance understated.

← Back to Black
Black · Black Orchid

Black Orchid

The black orchid is the boldest bloom we grow — near-black petals, wide and waxen, with a fine pale vein traced through each fold like a signature. Tall and unmissable when open, it's a flower that doesn't ask permission to be dramatic, and we wouldn't want it any other way.

← Back to White
White · Lily

Lily

There's nothing complicated about the lily, and that's exactly the point. White petals, a small gleam of gold at the centre, the kind of flower a child reaches for first without being taught to. We keep ours simple too — no dye, no drama, just the cheerful honesty ivory has always been known for.

← Back to Beige
Beige · Magnolia

Magnolia

The magnolia carries a colour that's hard to name — somewhere between sand, champagne, and the inside of a shell. It's the bloom we reach for when someone wants elegance without ceremony: soft, warm, and endlessly easy to carry in any hand, in any room.

← Back to Gold
Gold · Peony

Peony

Our peonies are left to open by hand until each layer takes on a soft, metallic gold — a fullness reserved for our most special blooms. They catch the light the way real celebration does: briefly, generously, and impossible to ignore. This is the flower we reach for when a moment deserves a little more shine.

← Back to Pink
Pink · Cherry Blossom

Cherry Blossom

For only a handful of days each year, the cherry trees let go of everything at once. What's left behind is a version of pink that doesn't really exist anywhere else — soft, brief, and slightly apologetic about how beautiful it is. We've tried to bottle that same feeling: fleeting, gentle, gone before you've finished looking.
